II · Poetical Science · running live

Motion study

How the film moves, and why

The film above is a scroll-driven website: one pinned 16:9 stage, ten keyframes and nine transitions, about a minute and a half played straight through. Every motion in it is built from a mechanism Lovelace described or could have watched: the shuttle and card prism of Jacquard’s loom, the Store and carry of Babbage’s engines, the pendulum. When the film rests on a keyframe the scene keeps going, and in IX, Pitched Sounds, with sound on, it is heard. Two scenes break the frame and fill it edge to edge. This study records the research, the timing, the loops, the sound and the reason for each transition.

Research

Five findings that set the rules

One card, one pick

On a Jacquard loom the card chain runs over a square prism. Each quarter-turn presents a new card; where it has a hole, a needle passes and its hook is lifted with the warp thread it controls. One card governs one throw of the shuttle.

Every reveal advances one row at a time, and anything card-like moves with the prism’s quarter-turn: quick, a small overshoot, settled.

Jacquard machine

Give off, then carry

In Babbage’s Difference Engine No. 2 each half-cycle first adds every figure at once, then deals with carries one position at a time from the lowest figure up. A carry can ripple through a run of nines.

The Store’s figures are set in that order: the bottom disc turns first and each turn passes upward.

Technical description, ed-thelen.org

Motion from stills, 1833–34

When Lovelace was eighteen, Plateau’s phenakistiscope (1832–33) and Horner’s Daedaleum (described in January 1834) made motion from a ring of still drawings seen through slits. Sources disagree on the exact years.

The film was designed stills first, as those devices teach: keyframes, then the in-betweens, then the loops that run while a still is held.

Zoetrope · History of Information

Two pendulums, one figure

The harmonograph draws with two pendulums, one per axis. Simple frequency ratios close into simple figures. It is usually credited to Hugh Blackburn in the 1840s, though the attribution is uncertain.

Released things swing past rest and settle, and every musical ratio in the film is drawn at the pendulums’ own pace.

Harmonograph

Pinned stage, scrubbed time

Scroll-driven storytelling pins a stage while the scroll scrubs a timeline; scrolling back reverses the motion. Good practice pairs every effect with a reduced-motion branch. The form is usually traced to the New York Times’ Snow Fall (2012).

The scroll is the crank handle. With reduced motion the stage steps between keyframes, the loops stop and nothing animates.

Tempo

Counted in beats

One beat is 480 milliseconds, 125 to the minute. Holds run six to sixteen beats and transitions eight to fourteen, so the film can be cut to music without retiming, and the synthesised sound sits on the same grid. Loops

Scenes that keep moving

A keyframe is where the scroll can rest, but the scene does not stop. An ambient clock runs underneath, and each scene has its own loop, faded in one beat after the transition lands and out one beat before the next, so every transition still starts from the exact keyframe. Sound

Only IX is heard

Sound is off until you press Sound. In this version the film is silent except for IX, Pitched Sounds, the scene where Note A imagines the Engine composing. As the roll is punched, each hole sounds its note; while the scene rests, the roll plays the numerators of the Bernoulli numbers in just intonation over a G drone, with a soft bed of filtered white noise beneath, like the hiss of an old recording. The sound comes in as IX arrives and dies away as the pendulums come to rest. Nothing is a recording: every sound is synthesised in the browser from the same timeline that draws the frames. The version with sound in every scene is kept under Full sound.

Exports

All exports come from the same engine as the site, in motion/exports/ of the project folder.

  • keyframes/K01_portrait.png … K10_no-pretensions.png: ten keyframes, 1920 × 1080 PNG, loops at rest.
  • boards/T0_…png … T9_…png: ten motion boards, 1920 × 1080 PNG.
  • Ada-Lovelace_storyboard.png: all ten keyframes on one sheet.
  • Ada-Lovelace_deck.pdf: twenty 16:9 pages, keyframes and boards in film order, 13.33 × 7.5 in, the widescreen slide size.
  • video/Ada-Lovelace_1080p30.mp4: the whole film with its soundtrack, heard only in IX; video/T1_…mp4 …: each transition with half a second of hold either side.
  • loops/K01_portrait_loop.mp4 …: each scene’s living loop at full strength, with its sound, for slides.
  • audio/Ada-Lovelace_soundtrack.wav: the soundtrack alone. The full-sound version keeps its own exports in motion-full-sound/exports/.

.venv\Scripts\python motion\tools\export.py regenerates every file.
